Losses are shown as positive numbers. Each horizon uses its own returns over whole days, months or quarters. Nothing is scaled up from daily, so quarterly figures rest on fewer observations.

How this is computed

VaR is the loss that is exceeded on only a small share of periods. CVaR is the average loss on the periods beyond it. Historical uses the observed returns. Gaussian uses the average and the spread of the returns. Cornish-Fisher adjusts the Gaussian figure for skew and fat tails.

A figure is marked indicative when Cornish-Fisher lands far from the historical figure. It is shown, but it should not be read as precise. The band holds the middle 90% of 1,000 resampled figures.
